# Limitations

This benchmark supports a specific engineering conclusion: on these frozen cases and this recorded binding, the integrated substrate produced more correct or executable outputs than the same binding with less context and capability. It does not by itself establish general intelligence, consciousness, personhood, or universal superiority.

Key limitations:

- The reference evidence is one deterministic campaign, not a multi-seed statistical sample.
- The batteries are small (`17 + 14 + 15` cases) and intentionally targeted at mechanisms the substrate claims to provide.
- One model/inference binding was used in the reference campaign. Cross-model generalization remains untested here.
- Objective substring matchers in `justesse` and `orchestration` are suitable only for opaque or tightly specified signals; they are not semantic judges.
- Two `justesse` cases remain judge-pending and are excluded from objective rates.
- The information-only arm is a counterfactual control, not a deployable product configuration. It isolates access from orchestration imperfectly because formatting information can itself influence inference.
- The orchestration family uses controlled fixtures and a proxy for some long-timescale mechanisms. It is not a longitudinal field study.
- Tool-chain cases use synthetic, opaque tokens and planted local data. They measure causal tool use and exactness, not open-world usefulness.
- Deterministic generation improves replayability but does not reveal outcome variance under stochastic decoding.
- The public package excludes binary runtime proofs and private state. Published JSON commitments are independently checkable, while excluded internals require a controlled audit of the original system.
- A third party can reproduce the protocol without Elyne, but reproducing the `core-origin` reference behavior requires implementing or connecting a substrate with the tested capabilities.

Future work should add preregistered batteries, multiple bindings, repeated seeds where stochastic decoding is relevant, blinded external judging, adversarial item generation, confidence intervals, and independent replications on third-party substrates.
